01

Bosch Future Mobility Challenge

Aalto team · 2026

The goal of the BFMC competition is to build an autonomous RC car that is able to navigate the track built by Bosch. Our car could lane-keep, recognize traffic signs and pedestrians using a YOLO model, localize itself with a combination of GPS, IMU and wheel encoder data, perform parking maneuvers and much more.

The Aalto 2026 team qualified for the finals in Romania, where we placed 14th — we did not manage a top-8 finish, but the trip was great and we had a good time. The full standings are on the Bosch Future Mobility Challenge results page.

  • Autonomous driving
  • YOLO
  • GPS + IMU
  • Wheel odometry
  • Lane keeping

03

Drive-by-wire kit

Mechatronics project course · 2025

A ROS 2 based system that allows for operation of a vehicle's wheel and pedals with a digital input. The system uses a rotary actuator to turn the wheel, and linear actuators to operate the gas and the brake pedal. The input used for the showcase video is a gamepad controller. YOLO based machine vision control was also performed. The system achieved reasonable control over a Saab 900 in slow speed operation; limited actuator speed on the wheel and pedals hinders current performance.

This drive-by-wire kit was part of the Mechatronics project course of 2025. A memorable visit to Tallinn to attend the Modern Materials & Manufacturing 2025 conference, where we showcased the results of the project, was part of the experience.

  • ROS 2
  • YOLO
  • Rotary + linear actuators
  • Saab 900
04

RC car wind tunnel

A wind tunnel built with a four-point scale to measure the weight and the effect of downforce on each wheel. The tunnel was powered by an automotive cooling fan, achieving wind speeds of around 10 m/s. An Arduino, in combination with four strain gauges, was used to measure the individual wheels, as well as to perform PWM control over a MOSFET in order to control wind speed.

The obtained data clearly showed the aerodynamic effect on the tested RC cars. Surprisingly, a conventional passenger-vehicle-shaped RC car ended up creating lift instead of downforce, and adding a wing only reduced the lift. The formula style car shown in the photo was the only tested RC car to create downforce.

  • Arduino
  • Strain gauges
  • MOSFET PWM
  • Aerodynamics
